# Dissertation level (M14 and what changes)

For your own doctoral work. The same modules apply at a higher bar, plus M14.

## What changes from S1

- **M13 contribution** is judged on originality: what is known after this work that was not
  known before, and is that stated in one sentence in the introduction and the conclusion?
- **M04 literature** must show the field's current state, not a textbook account. "No study has
  done X" needs a documented search behind it.
- **M09 claims** get read by examiners who know the area. Every claim in the abstract and
  conclusion should trace to a result, and every result to a script or results file.

## M14 Dissertation thread and publications

A dissertation by publication, or a monograph built from published articles, fails in ways a
single article does not. Check:

1. **One overarching question.** Can the dissertation's research question be stated in one
   sentence, and does every chapter answer a part of it? A chapter that answers a different
   question is a finding even if the article behind it is good.
2. **Chapter ↔ article map.** For each chapter: which article, its status (published,
   submitted, draft), and what the chapter adds beyond the article (connecting text, a result
   that did not fit the page limit). An introduction that only restates the articles' abstracts
   does not synthesise.
3. **Consistency across articles.** The same dataset described with different N, filters, or
   periods; the same term defined differently; a later article's result that silently
   contradicts an earlier one. Say which is right, and whether the earlier chapter needs a note.
4. **Numbers trace to sources.** Every number should come from a results file, script output,
   or the published article — never retyped from memory. If the project keeps results files
   (e.g. `Hasil_*.md`), check chapter numbers against them.
5. **Reuse and self-plagiarism.** Text reused from your own published articles needs the
   publisher's permission where required, and a statement in the chapter ("This chapter is
   based on …"). Check each publisher's policy on reuse in a thesis; most allow it with credit.
6. **Co-authored chapters.** State your contribution for each co-authored article (CRediT roles
   are the common form). Examiners and many universities require it.
7. **Earlier work that no longer holds.** If later work showed an earlier result was wrong or
   weaker than stated, the dissertation must say so. Silence is worse than a correction written
   by the author.

## Using it on a proposal or concept note

Early on there is no dissertation to audit, but the chain already exists in the proposal and
the concept note sent to supervisors: question → gap → data → method → expected contribution →
planned articles. Prepare a workspace on the proposal at level `disertasi` and run M01–M05, M09,
M13, M14; mark the rest `not_applicable` with the reason "proposal stage". The most useful
check at this stage is whether the proposal still matches the research actually being done.
