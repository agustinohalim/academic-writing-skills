# After a Decision

## Reading a rejection

First decide which gate the paper failed:

| Signal | Gate | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Decision within days, no reviewer reports | Desk (editor) | Scope, contribution, visible flaw, abstract, or non-compliance |
| Reviewer reports attached | Review | The science or its presentation did not convince |

Editors usually give three or more reasons at once, even when the letter names one. Map every
sentence of the letter to the pre-submission scorecard (`scientific-article-writing`), then
decide:

- **Scope only** (the letter names another field or readership, or offers a transfer): move to
  the next rung with the paper unchanged except for journal formatting.
- **Contribution, importance, or novelty**: do **not** move down the ladder unchanged. Rework the
  title, abstract, Introduction, and contribution list; check the "solves nothing" trap
  (`editor-and-reviewer-criteria.md`). A lower rung's editor asks the same question.
- **Method or analysis**: fix it, re-run from scripts, then move on.
- **Presentation or language**: fix with `manuscript-proofreading`; this alone rarely explains a
  rejection.

Record the decision, date, manuscript ID, and reasons in the ladder file before acting.

## Transfer offers

Elsevier's Article Transfer Service (other publishers run similar schemes):

- After a rejection, the editor or a matching system may recommend one or more journals from the
  same publisher. **Nothing is transferred unless the author accepts.**
- On accepting, the manuscript — and the reviewer reports, if any — goes to the chosen journal.
  The author can revise on the reviews **before** launching the transfer, and must then log into
  the new journal to complete anything it requires.
- Some offers combine a rejection with an offer of acceptance in another journal, usually with
  minor revisions.

Before accepting, put the proposed journal through Steps 2–3 of the skill exactly like a ladder
rung (fit, index status, required metric, APC). A transfer is a convenience, not an
endorsement. Accept only if the journal would have qualified for the ladder on its own.

## Moving down the ladder

- Restyle references, re-check word limits and required sections, and re-read the new journal's
  AI policy. Do this from the Markdown source, then re-export.
- Address every reviewer comment, even though the next journal will not see them: the same
  reviewers may be invited again, and they will notice.
- Rewrite the cover letter for the new readership; never reuse one that names the previous
  journal.
- Update the manuscript status note and the status board; commit.

## Appeals

Appeal only when the decision rests on a demonstrable factual error (a reviewer misread a
number, a required analysis is already in the paper). Disagreement about importance is not
grounds. Elsevier Researcher Academy's module on appeals is titled *Appealing the rejection and
why you should think twice*.

## When to leave the ladder

Two rejections for the same reason (importance, scope, novelty) on different rungs mean the
problem is the paper's contribution, not the journals. Stop submitting and return to the
article plan: the one-sentence contribution and scorecard section A.
